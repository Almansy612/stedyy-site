Privacy Policy

Stedy, the weight-progression tracker for iPhone. Effective 4 October 2026, updated 25 November 2026.

Français

In short

  • Your workouts are stored on your iPhone. Without an account, they are never uploaded.
  • An account is optional. If you sign in, we store account details such as your name and email address, and a backup of your workouts and your About you answers, so you can get them back on a new iPhone (see below).
  • Your photos, and the Measurements and Posture readings taken from them, are worked out on your iPhone and stay there.
  • The Assistant runs on your iPhone. Your questions aren't sent anywhere.
  • If you turn it on, Stedy saves your finished workouts to Apple Health on your iPhone. It never reads your health data.
  • Premium is paid through Apple. RevenueCat, our subscription service, checks whether you have it, without your name, your account or your workouts (see Subscriptions).
  • No ads and no tracking. Expo, which delivers app updates, counts active installs with a random ID (see App updates). We never sell your data.
  • Speed and error reports are sent only if you allow them, and never include your workouts. You can turn them off at any time in Preferences.

Data that stays on your iPhone

Everything you log in Stedy is stored on your iPhone: your workouts, sets, weights and reps, your exercises and day types, how a session felt, your plan, and the About you and setup answers you give (such as your unit, goal, experience, body weight, height, date of birth, sex and build). Your Assistant conversations are also stored only on your iPhone. Without an account we never receive any of it. If you sign in, a backup of it (not your Assistant conversations, photos, Measurements or Posture) is kept in your account, as described under Account backup.

  • Your photos. Your profile photo and your progress photos are kept inside Stedy on your iPhone. They are never uploaded and are not in any Stedy backup. Stedy asks before using the camera; choosing a photo from your library uses Apple's photo picker, so Stedy sees only the photo you pick.
  • Measurements and Posture. If you use them, Stedy's camera takes photos of you, and Apple's on-device image analysis finds the outline and position of your body to estimate your shoulder and waist widths, and whether your shoulders and hips sit level. This happens on your iPhone. The photos and the numbers are stored only on your iPhone, are not in any Stedy backup, and are never used for advertising. They are estimates, not medical measurements.
  • Your age. Stedy uses the date of birth you save to check, on your iPhone, that you're old enough: 13 to use the app and 14 to sign in. It also shapes the wording of training tips. In a few places the law has apps check a person's age group (when this was written: Texas, Utah and Louisiana in the United States, for Apple Accounts created there since mid-2026). There, Stedy also asks your iPhone for the age group Apple holds for your Apple Account, such as "13 to 15" or "18 or over", and uses it for the same things. That age group stays in the app's memory on your iPhone while Stedy is open: it is not saved, not added to any backup, not sent to us and not shared with anyone.
  • Sharing. Share cards, recaps and photos leave your iPhone only when you share or save them yourself. A photo you put behind a share card is used on your iPhone and isn't kept. If you tap Instagram Stories, Stedy hands the picture to the Instagram app on your iPhone, with an ID that tells Instagram it came from Stedy; what Instagram does with it is covered by Meta's privacy policy.
  • Backups you make, and imports. If you export a backup, the file goes wherever you choose to save or send it. We don't receive a copy. A file you import, from Stedy or from another app, is read on your iPhone.
  • Notifications, widgets and the Live Activity. The end-of-rest and trial reminders are scheduled on your iPhone; nothing is sent to us to deliver them. Widgets and the Live Activity read their data from your iPhone.
  • iCloud Backup. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud, Apple may include Stedy's data, photos among it, in that backup under Apple's own terms. We can't read it.
  • Deleting. Deleting the app deletes this data from your iPhone. If you're signed in, your account backup stays in your account until you delete the account.

The Assistant

The Assistant uses Apple's on-device model (Apple Intelligence) to answer questions about your training. Your questions, your logs and its answers are processed on your iPhone and are not sent to us or to anyone else.

Apple Health

Saving workouts to Apple Health is off until you turn it on in Stedy, and iOS asks for your permission first. When it's on, each workout you finish is saved to the Health app on your iPhone as strength training, with its start and end time. Stedy only writes to Health: it doesn't read any of your health data, and nothing from Health is uploaded, used for advertising or shared with anyone. You can turn it off in Preferences at any time, and remove Stedy's access in your iPhone's Settings, under Health.

Optional account

You can use Stedy without an account. Accounts are for people 14 or older, so Stedy asks for your date of birth before you sign in and checks it on your iPhone. If you choose to sign in with Apple or Google, our sign-in provider, Supabase, stores:

  • your name, as Apple or Google shares it with us;
  • your email address (with Apple, this can be a private relay address that hides your real one);
  • with Google, a link to your Google profile photo;
  • which service you signed in with, and an account ID.

If you sign in, Stedy also keeps a backup in your account (see Account backup below). Supabase also records the IP address and device type of each signed-in session; these go when you sign out or delete your account. It also keeps technical logs of requests, sign-in requests among them, which can include IP addresses, for a short time (between one and seven days, depending on our plan), then deletes them automatically. Signing in is also handled by Apple or Google under their own privacy policies.

Deleting your account: in Stedy, tap your photo at the top left of Home, open your profile, and under Account tap Delete account. This permanently removes your account, its sessions and your account backup from Supabase, and removes Stedy from your Apple or Google sign-in. Your workouts stay on your iPhone. Recent logs expire on their own, as described above.

Account backup

If you're signed in, Stedy keeps a backup of your data in your account, so you can get it back on a new iPhone or after reinstalling the app. After each workout you finish, after you import workouts, and when you sign in, Stedy uploads one backup file to Supabase. It contains:

  • your logged workouts: dates, exercises, sets, weights and reps, how long a session took, how it felt, and whether you ticked creatine;
  • your exercises and day types, and your plan: your training days, days off, and the order of your workouts;
  • your About you and setup answers: unit, goal, experience, date of birth, sex, height, body weight and its history, and build;
  • app choices, such as your theme.

It doesn't contain your Assistant conversations, your profile photo, your progress photos, or your Measurements and Posture; those stay only on your iPhone.

The file is stored privately under your account ID. Through the app, only you, signed in, can read it. We don't look at backups or use them for anything else, and we never share or sell them. There is one copy, replaced each time. When you sign in on an iPhone, Stedy downloads it and adds anything that's missing there; nothing on that iPhone is deleted. Signing out keeps the backup in your account; deleting your account deletes it.

App updates

Stedy checks Expo's update service for small app updates. That request sends technical information, such as the app version and the device platform, and a random ID for this installation, made when the app first opens. It isn't made from your device, your Apple Account or your Stedy account. Expo stores that ID and your IP address with a record of each request, and uses them to count active installs and failed updates. If an update fails to start, the error message goes to Expo, which keeps it in its logs for three days. None of this includes your workouts or your account.

Speed and error reports

Stedy can send reports that help us find screens that are slow or break. Nothing is sent unless you allow it. Stedy asks once, on Home after setup and never during a workout: "Allow "Stedy" to send speed and error reports?" You can change your answer at any time in Preferences, under Reports. While reports are off, nothing is collected for later.

If you allow them, Stedy sends to Expo, our app-services provider:

  • how long the app takes to start, and how long each screen takes to appear, with the screen's name;
  • the app version, your iPhone model, iOS version and language, and device conditions such as Low Power Mode, battery level and charging, temperature state and network type;
  • at launch, the number and size of the app's internet requests, and which service the slowest one went to;
  • when something goes wrong in the app or it crashes, the error message and where in the app's code it happened;
  • a random ID for this installation, and one for each time the app is opened, which let reports be grouped together. They aren't made from your device, your Apple Account or your Stedy account, and the installation's ID changes if you reinstall the app (restoring an iPhone backup can bring the previous one back).

Expo also stores the IP address each report comes from, and the country worked out from it. Reports never include your workouts, your exercises or their names, your setup answers, your Assistant conversations or your account details, and they aren't linked to your account. We use them only to find and fix problems in Stedy.

Subscriptions

Stedy Premium is a subscription sold through the App Store. Apple takes the payment under its own terms; Stedy never sees your payment details. To know whether you have Premium, Stedy uses RevenueCat, our subscription service. Each time Stedy opens, and when you buy or restore Premium, it sends RevenueCat:

  • a random ID that RevenueCat makes the first time Stedy opens. It isn't made from your name, your email address, your Apple Account or your Stedy account;
  • the identifier Apple gives your iPhone for apps from one developer (the "identifier for vendor"), which RevenueCat uses to check App Store receipts. It isn't your name or your Apple Account, and other developers' apps get a different one;
  • your App Store purchases of Stedy Premium, if any: the plan, its price and currency, and when it started, renews or ended;
  • technical information: the app version, the iOS version, your iPhone model, its language and region, your App Store country, and whether your iPhone allows apps to ask to track you (Stedy never asks).

RevenueCat uses the IP address of the request to work out your country, then doesn't keep the address. None of this includes your workouts, your setup answers, your Assistant conversations or your account details, and it isn't linked to your Stedy account. We use it only to turn Premium on, to restore it with your Apple Account, and to count subscriptions. It's never used for ads.

Apple and TestFlight

If you share app analytics with developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may give us anonymous crash and usage reports. If you test Stedy through TestFlight, Apple shares the crash reports and feedback you choose to send. Apple handles this under its own privacy policy.

Who handles your data, and where

Supabase (accounts and account backups), Expo (app updates, and speed and error reports) and RevenueCat (subscriptions) are service providers. They handle your data only for us, under data processing agreements that require them to keep it confidential and secure and to use it only to provide their service: the same protection this policy gives it. We share your data with no one else. Apple and Google provide sign-in under their own privacy policies, and Instagram receives a picture only when you share one to it.

Supabase stores account data and account backups in Canada (Montreal). Its technical logs are stored in the European Union, and its delivery network and support staff may handle data in other countries, including the United States. Expo and RevenueCat are in the United States. This means your information may be communicated outside Quebec and outside Canada, where it is subject to the laws of those places.

How long we keep data

  • Account data (name, email address, Google profile photo link, sign-in service and account ID): until you delete your account.
  • Account backup (only if you sign in): one copy, replaced each time, kept until you delete your account.
  • Session records (IP address and device type): until you sign out or delete your account.
  • Supabase's technical logs (which can include IP addresses): between one and seven days, depending on our plan, then deleted automatically.
  • App update records (the random installation ID and IP address): kept by Expo to count installs; error messages from failed updates, three days.
  • Speed and error reports (only if you allow them): kept by Expo for about three months (up to 93 days), then deleted.
  • Subscription records (RevenueCat's IDs and your Premium purchases): kept by RevenueCat while Stedy uses it, so Premium can be restored. They aren't linked to your name or account; to ask for them to be deleted, email us.

Security

Data sent between Stedy and the services above travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Your sign-in is kept in your iPhone's Keychain, and your account backup can be read only with your sign-in. No system is completely secure. If a security incident puts your personal information at risk, we'll tell you and the privacy authorities, as the law requires.

What we don't do

  • No advertising and no ad tracking.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites, and no advertising analytics. Expo's install count, RevenueCat's subscription check and the optional speed and error reports are explained above.
  • We never sell or share your data with data brokers.
  • We never use your health or fitness data, or your photos, for advertising or marketing.

Your rights and choices

You can see and change everything you've logged in the app itself, and delete it by deleting the app. You can take back a permission at any time: sign out or delete your account (which deletes your account backup) from your profile in Stedy; turn off Apple Health and speed and error reports in Preferences; and turn off the camera, Health and notifications for Stedy in your iPhone's Settings. Reports already sent aren't linked to you, so they can't be matched to you for deletion, and are removed when Expo's retention period ends.

You can ask us what personal information we hold about you, for a copy of it, or to have it corrected or deleted: email steedy.help@gmail.com and we'll reply within 30 days. You can also complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or, in Quebec, the Commission d'accès à l'information.
