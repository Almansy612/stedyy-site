Privacy Policy

Stedy, the weight-progression tracker for iPhone. Effective 4 October 2026, updated 27 October 2026.

In short

  • Your workouts are stored on your iPhone. Without an account, they are never uploaded.
  • An account is optional. If you sign in, we store account details such as your name and email address, records of your sign-ins, and a backup of your workouts so you can get them back on a new iPhone (see below).
  • The Assistant runs on your iPhone. Your questions aren't sent anywhere.
  • If you turn it on, Stedy saves your finished workouts to Apple Health on your iPhone. It never reads your health data.
  • No ads and no tracking. Expo, which delivers app updates, counts active installs with a random ID (see App updates). We never sell your data.
  • Speed and error reports are sent only if you allow them, and never include your workouts. You can turn them off at any time in Preferences.

Who we are

Stedy is made by Abdallah Almansy, an independent developer ("we"). If you have a question about your data, email steedy.help@gmail.com.

Person in charge of personal information: Abdallah Almansy, steedy.help@gmail.com.

Data that stays on your iPhone

Everything you log in Stedy is stored on your iPhone: your workouts, sets, weights and reps, your exercises and day types, how a session felt, and the setup answers you give (such as your unit, goal, experience, body weight, date of birth, sex and build). Your Assistant conversations are also stored only on your iPhone. Without an account we never receive any of it. If you sign in, a backup of it (not your Assistant conversations or photos) is kept in your account, as described under Account backup.

  • Backups you make. If you export a backup, the file goes wherever you choose to save or send it. We don't receive a copy.
  • Photos for share cards. If you take or choose a photo as the background of a share card, Stedy uses it on your iPhone only and doesn't keep it. It leaves your iPhone only if you share the card. Stedy asks before using the camera; choosing from your library uses Apple's photo picker, so Stedy sees only the photo you pick.
  • iCloud Backup. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud, Apple may include Stedy's data in that backup under Apple's own terms. We can't read it.
  • Deleting. Deleting the app deletes this data from your iPhone. If you're signed in, your account backup stays in your account until you delete the account.

The Assistant

The Assistant uses Apple's on-device model (Apple Intelligence) to answer questions about your training. Your questions, your logs and its answers are processed on your iPhone and are not sent to us or to anyone else.

Apple Health

Saving workouts to Apple Health is off until you turn it on in Stedy's Preferences, and iOS asks for your permission first. When it's on, each workout you finish is saved to the Health app on your iPhone as strength training, with its start and end time. Stedy only writes to Health: it doesn't read any of your health data, and nothing from Health is uploaded, used for advertising or shared with anyone. You can turn it off in Preferences at any time, and remove Stedy's access in your iPhone's Settings, under Health.

Optional account

You can use Stedy without an account. If you choose to sign in with Apple or Google, our sign-in provider, Supabase, stores:

  • your name, as Apple or Google shares it with us;
  • your email address (with Apple, this can be a private relay address that hides your real one);
  • with Google, a link to your Google profile photo;
  • which service you signed in with, and an account ID.

If you sign in, Stedy also keeps a backup of your workouts in your account (see Account backup below). Supabase also records the IP address and device type of each signed-in session; these go when you sign out or delete your account. It also keeps logs of sign-in requests, with IP addresses, for a short time (an hour on our current plan), then deletes them automatically. Signing in is also handled by Apple or Google under their own privacy policies.

Deleting your account: in Stedy, open your profile (the round button at the top left of Home) and tap Delete account. This permanently removes your account, its sessions and your account backup from Supabase. Your workouts stay on your iPhone. Recent sign-in logs expire on their own, as described above.

Account backup

If you're signed in, Stedy keeps a backup of your data in your account, so you can get it back on a new iPhone or after reinstalling the app. After each workout you finish, and when you tap Back up now in Preferences, Stedy uploads one backup file to Supabase. It contains your logged workouts, sets, weights and reps, your exercises and day types, how your sessions felt, and your setup answers (such as your unit, goal, experience, training days, date of birth, sex, height, body weight and build). It doesn't contain your Assistant conversations, your profile photo or your progress photos; those stay only on your iPhone.

The file is stored privately under your account ID. Through the app, only you, signed in, can read it. We don't look at backups or use them for anything else, and we never share or sell them. There is one copy, replaced each time. When you sign in on an iPhone, Stedy downloads it and adds anything that's missing there; nothing on that iPhone is deleted. Signing out keeps the backup in your account; deleting your account deletes it.

App updates

Stedy checks Expo's update service for small app updates. That request sends technical information, such as the app version and the device platform, along with your IP address, as any internet request does, and a random ID for this installation, made when the app first opens, which Expo uses to count active installs. If an update fails to start, a report about that error goes to Expo. None of this includes your workouts or your account.

Speed and error reports

Stedy can send reports that help us find screens that are slow or break. Nothing is sent unless you allow it. Stedy asks once, on Home after setup and never during a workout: "Allow "Stedy" to send speed and error reports?" You can change your answer at any time in Preferences, under Reports. While reports are off, nothing is collected for later.

If you allow them, Stedy sends to Expo, our app-services provider:

  • how long the app takes to start, and how long each screen takes to appear;
  • the app version, your iPhone model and iOS version, and device conditions such as Low Power Mode, battery level and charging, temperature state and network type;
  • at launch, the number and size of the app's internet requests, and which service the slowest one went to;
  • when something goes wrong in the app, the error message and where in the app's code it happened;
  • a random ID for this installation, which lets reports from the same installation be grouped together. It isn't made from your device, your Apple Account or your Stedy account, and it changes if you reinstall the app (restoring an iPhone backup can bring the previous one back).

Like any internet request, sending a report also reveals your IP address to Expo. Reports never include your workouts, your exercises or their names, your setup answers, your Assistant conversations or your account details, and they aren't linked to your account. We use them only to find and fix problems in Stedy.

Apple and TestFlight

If you share app analytics with developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may give us anonymous crash and usage reports. If you test Stedy through TestFlight, Apple shares the crash reports and feedback you choose to send. Apple handles this under its own privacy policy.

Who handles your data, and where

Supabase and Expo process data for us, and Apple and Google provide sign-in, each under their own privacy and security terms. Supabase stores account data and account backups in Canada (Montreal). Expo, which runs the update service and receives speed and error reports, is in the United States. Your data may be processed outside Canada.

How long we keep data

  • Account data (name, email address, Google profile photo link, sign-in service and account ID): until you delete your account.
  • Account backup (only if you sign in): one copy, replaced after each workout, kept until you delete your account.
  • Session records (IP address and device type): until you sign out or delete your account.
  • Sign-in logs (with IP addresses): kept by Supabase for a short time (an hour on our current plan), then deleted automatically.
  • Speed and error reports (only if you allow them): kept by Expo for at least 60 days; Expo's current plans keep them for 90 days.

Security

Data sent between Stedy and the services above travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). No system is completely secure.

What we don't do

  • No advertising and no ad tracking.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites, and no advertising analytics. Expo's install count and the optional speed and error reports are explained above.
  • We never sell or share your data with data brokers.

Your rights

You can see and change everything you've logged in the app itself, and delete it by deleting the app. You can delete your account, and with it your account backup, at any time from your profile in Stedy. You can stop speed and error reports at any time in Preferences, under Reports; reports already sent aren't linked to you, so they can't be matched to you for deletion and are removed when Expo's retention period ends. To ask what account data we hold, or for anything else about your data, email steedy.help@gmail.com and we'll reply within 30 days. You can also complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or, in Quebec, the Commission d'accès à l'information.

Children

Stedy is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal data from them. Using the app doesn't require an account. In Quebec, you must be 14 or older to sign in, or have a parent's or guardian's consent.

Changes to this policy

If Stedy starts handling data differently, for example when paid subscriptions are added, we'll update this page and the date at the top before the change reaches the app.
